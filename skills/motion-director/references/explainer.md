# Explainers

An explainer leaves the viewer able to say how something works or why it is so. The motion *is* the explanation:
every shot has a verb (gather, split, align, connect, filter, reveal), and the thing that changes is the point.

- **One question, answered.** Write the question in one line (it is often the title) and the answer as one change
  the viewer can watch happen. In a trial that is one or two shots, each with one verb; a full explanation in steps
  is the Explainer Video skill's film.
- **The object carries the film.** Pick one object the viewer follows from start to end (a document, a request, a
  packet, a coin, the product's icon) and move *it* through the steps. A film of slides that replace each other is
  not an explainer.
- **Style:** whiteboard (the idea drawn as it is explained) or kinetic type (the claim landing word by word); the
  `whiteboard` grammar is a fast start.
- **Rules are shown, not listed.** "It asks before it sends" is the request stopping at a gate and waiting for a
  hand; a list of rules on screen is the fallback, not the film.
- **Pace for understanding.** One idea, built in 6–10 s, then held long enough to read.
- **End on the answer** in one line, then the source.
